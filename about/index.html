About the map and its data

GlobeFront draws one globe for every view, from the whole Earth down to a single strait. Every layer on it comes from a named source, listed below with what it allows.

Boundaries and disputed areas

Country outlines and boundary lines come from Natural Earth (version 5.1.2), which shows political boundaries by their de facto status, who controls the ground, rather than by legal claim, and marks disputed boundaries. GlobeFront uses Natural Earth’s default view and draws disputed, indefinite and line-of-control boundaries dashed. See Natural Earth’s disputed boundaries policy.

Sources on the map

  • Imagery: NASA Blue Marble shaded relief and bathymetry, via NASA GIBS (Global Imagery Browse Services)
  • Night lights: NASA Earth Observatory / NOAA NGDC, VIIRS city lights 2012, via NASA GIBS
  • Terrain: © Mapterhorn, built on open elevation data including Copernicus GLO-30
  • Boundaries and water areas: Natural Earth (public domain). Boundaries are drawn de facto; disputed lines are dashed.
  • Charted lanes near the Strait of Hormuz: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Only the two sections OpenStreetMap maps are drawn; not for navigation.
  • Country facts: World Bank Open Data, CC BY 4.0. Each figure shows its indicator and year.
  • Strait of Hormuz location: Marine Regions gazetteer (CC BY 4.0), checked against Wikidata.
